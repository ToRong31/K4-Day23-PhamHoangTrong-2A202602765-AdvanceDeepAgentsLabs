# World Models: Approaches, Evidence and Open Problems

## TL;DR
- The literature is dominated by two traditions that barely cite each other: control-oriented latent dynamics models that predict a compressed state for imagination or planning [1][2][3], and large-scale generative video models that predict future observations and are presented as learned simulators of the world [4][5][6].
- Both traditions share one recipe — compress observations into a predictive state, roll it forward conditioned on actions, then either train a policy inside the model or optimise actions at test time; the variants differ mainly in the prediction target (pixels, latents, pre-trained features, or discrete tokens) and in whether the model is mechanically interactive [7][8][9][10].
- Evidence of benefit is strongest in closed-loop control, where single-configuration agents generalise broadly: DreamerV3 is reported to solve over 150 tasks with one configuration [3][11] and TD-MPC2 to train a single 317M-parameter agent for 80 tasks [12], while DayDreamer trains a real quadruped to stand and walk in only one hour of real-robot time [13].
- Generative video world models mostly report playability and perceptual metrics rather than physics: GameNGen claims 20 FPS and a next-frame PSNR of 29.4 [5], while the system with the loudest simulation claims (Sora) also documents its own physics failures [6], and a physics-adherence benchmark puts the best tested generator at 39.6% adherence [14].
- Evaluation is the weakest link: two 2026 meta-reviews of 160 and 102 benchmarks find that prediction is rarely executed, that world-model-vs-policy contrasts are rare, and that open-loop scores are overinterpreted as closed-loop evidence [15][16].

## Background
A world model is a learned internal predictor of environment dynamics that an agent can use in place of, or in addition to, the real environment — for planning, for policy learning in imagination, or for both [1][2]. The classical construction separates perception, a recurrent dynamics model and a small controller, and demonstrates that a policy trained entirely inside the model transfers back to the true environment [1][17]. Model-based reinforcement learning formalises the motivation: learning dynamics first and then planning with the learned model is meant to buy sample efficiency over model-free learning [18]. What counts as a world model, however, is genuinely contested. One survey argues that existing reviews "lack a systematic examination of what precisely constitutes a world model" and organises the field around two functions, namely constructing internal representations to understand the world and predicting future states to simulate and guide decision-making [19]; another states that the "absence of a standardized definition and taxonomy for 'world models' has led to fragmented and sometimes inconsistent claims", and imposes its own VideoGen/OccGen/LiDARGen split with common comparison axes of fidelity, consistency, controllability and scalability [20]. A 2026 survey re-frames world models as "internal simulators" spanning reinforcement learning, robotics, driving and video generation and proposes a four-dimension taxonomy precisely because the field "lacks a unified framework" [21]. The topic matters now because the two traditions have met: video diffusion transformers trained at scale [6], interactive neural game engines [4][5], world-foundation-model platforms [22], and joint-embedding predictive models used as planners [8][9] all now claim the same "world model" label, so the definitional and evaluative questions have become practical ones.

## Theme 1: Latent-dynamics world models for control
The oldest line learns a low-dimensional latent state and predicts its evolution. World Models trains a variational encoder (V) and a Mixture-Density LSTM (M) "in an unsupervised manner to learn a compressed spatial and temporal representation", then trains a tiny controller (C) on the model's features and transfers it to the real environment [1]; its companion paper takes the same generative recurrent network and evolves compact policies on the extracted features [17]. PlaNet keeps latent-space planning but makes the latent both deterministic and stochastic in a recurrent state-space model, trained with a multi-step variational objective the authors call latent overshooting, and reports solving pixel-based continuous control "with contact dynamics, partial observability, and sparse rewards" while using "substantially fewer episodes" than strong model-free baselines — the paper's headline figure is comparable performance with 200x fewer episodes [2]. MuZero departs from reconstruction altogether: it learns a model that "predicts the quantities most directly relevant to planning: the reward, the action-selection policy, and the value function", combined with tree search, achieving superhuman results on 57 Atari games and matching AlphaZero without being given the rules of Go, chess and shogi [18].

Later work pushes this design in two directions — generalisation and predictive efficiency. DreamerV3 learns a compact latent state by autoencoding and improves behaviour by imagination, with an actor and critic reading out imagined outcomes, and is reported to outperform tuned specialists across over 150 tasks with a single configuration, including collecting diamonds in Minecraft from scratch without human data or curricula [3]; its journal version reports the protocol explicitly, evaluating 8 domains under fixed hyperparameters and giving baselines a 10x data advantage at 1 billion steps when they perform poorly at 100 million [11]. Recall-to-Imagine swaps the recurrent core for state-space models inside the world model and reports a new state of the art on memory and credit-assignment suites (BSuite, POPGym), superhuman performance on Memory Maze, and faster wall-time convergence than DreamerV3 [23]. TD-MPC and TD-MPC2 remove reconstruction entirely: a decoder-free, task-oriented latent dynamics model is optimised by local trajectory optimisation with a learned terminal value function, both learned jointly by temporal-difference learning, on the argument that modelling only what is "predictive of reward" is "a far easier problem" [24]; TD-MPC2 reports improvements across 104 online RL tasks in 4 domains with one hyperparameter set and a single 317M-parameter agent handling 80 tasks across embodiments while scaling its world model 300x [12]. In parallel, the theory side has begun to state what such models must satisfy: "predictive sufficiency" formalises how much access to context adds to next-step prediction under the visitation distribution an agent induces, separating a history-recoverable part, a residual requiring true context, and the deficit contributed by a finite model [25]. The implicit taxonomies are easy to compare — reconstruction-based versus reward-relevant-only prediction [24], stochastic-versus-deterministic latents [2], and actor-critic imagination [3] versus online planning [2][24] versus tree search [18] — and a 2025 survey formalises exactly this design space for embodied settings with axes for functionality (decision-coupled vs general-purpose), temporal modelling (sequential simulation vs global difference prediction) and spatial representation (latent vector, token sequence, latent grid, decomposed rendering) [26].

## Theme 2: Generative video world models as simulators
The second line predicts observations or tokens directly, at scale. GAIA-1 casts driving world modelling as "unsupervised sequence modeling" over discrete video, text and action tokens, predicting the next token and claiming emergent "high-level structures and scene dynamics, contextual awareness, generalization, and understanding of geometry" [7]; GAIA-2 moves to latent diffusion with structured conditioning (ego dynamics, agent configuration, environment factors, road semantics) and multi-camera consistency across UK, US and German environments, explicitly arguing that driving needs fine-grained control that generic text-to-video models do not provide [27]. Sora is the reference point for the "video generation is world simulation" thesis: a text-conditional diffusion transformer over spacetime latent patches whose 3D consistency, object permanence and interactivity are described as "purely phenomena of scale", with no explicit inductive bias for 3D — in the same report that admits it "does not accurately model the physics of many basic interactions, like glass shattering", that food state changes are often wrong, and that model and implementation details are withheld [6]. Genie instead makes interactivity the deliverable: an 11B-parameter model composed of a spatiotemporal video tokenizer, an autoregressive dynamics model and a latent action model, trained on unlabelled Internet videos yet controllable frame by frame, with the learned latent action space enabling imitation from unseen videos [4]. Within games, DIAMOND replaces discrete latents with diffusion and reports a mean human-normalised score of 1.46 on Atari 100k — described as a new best for agents trained entirely inside a world model — and can also serve as a stand-alone neural game engine [28]; GameNGen trains in two phases (an RL agent plays DOOM, then a diffusion model predicts the next frame conditioned on past frames and actions) and reports 20 FPS on a single TPU, next-frame PSNR 29.4, and human raters only slightly better than chance at telling clips from the simulation, though its evidence is stability and perceptual quality rather than physical accuracy [5]. Cosmos generalises the ambition into an open-weight platform — video curation pipeline, video tokenizers, and both diffusion and autoregressive transformer world foundation models over continuous and discrete latents, post-trainable for camera controllability, manipulation and driving — and claims generation of "high-quality 3D consistent videos with accurate physics", a claim that the retrieved text leaves qualitative [22]. The newest work pushes toward streaming, embodied and multi-agent deployment: SPW-Nav streams a minute of 2K 360-degree video in real time from a single panorama and interprets language instructions as camera motion [29]; a multi-agent egocentric world model formulates generation as synchronised ego-streams for several agents acting through fine-grained interactions, arguing earlier multi-agent models rely on coarse locomotion or discrete commands [30]; and Wonder targets real-time camera-controllable long-horizon exploration with dense coordinate conditioning, sparse-attention memory and self-forcing distillation [31]. Notably, the field's own flagship caution comes from the first of these papers' family: these systems are judged by perceptual and playability metrics, not by whether the predicted world obeys physics [5][6].

## Theme 3: Embodied, robotic and feature-space world models
Robotics is where world models are tested against reality. DayDreamer applies Dreamer to four real robots without simulators: a quadruped learns to roll off its back, stand and walk from scratch in one hour and adapts to pushes within ten minutes, and two arms learn pick-and-place from camera images and sparse rewards, approaching human performance, with predictions made over future representations rather than future inputs to reduce accumulating error [13]. Rather than reconstructing pixels, DINO-WM predicts future DINOv2 patch features from offline behavioural trajectories and then plans by optimising an action sequence toward a goal feature, achieving zero-shot behavioural solutions on six environment families (mazes, push manipulation, multi-particle scenarios) without expert demonstrations or learned inverse models [8]. V-JEPA 2 scales that idea: an action-free joint-embedding predictive architecture is pre-trained on over one million hours of internet video, then post-trained into an action-conditioned latent world model (V-JEPA 2-AC) using less than 62 hours of unlabelled Droid robot video, and deployed zero-shot on Franka arms in two different labs for image-goal pick-and-place without any robot data, task-specific training or reward, alongside perception results such as 77.3 top-1 on Something-Something v2 and 39.7 recall-at-5 on Epic-Kitchens-100 [9]. Hybrid designs also appear: MoWM fuses motion-aware latent representations as a high-level prior with a pixel-space model for fine-grained decoding and reports state-of-the-art task success on CALVIN [10], while ChronoDreamer proposes an action-conditioned, token-based world model as an online simulator for robotic planning, judging predicted rollouts with a vision-language model that reasons about collision likelihood [32]. The 2026 World-Action Model survey names this emerging category directly — models that "couple future world prediction with executable action generation" — and scopes it against conventional world models, model-based RL, action-conditioned video generation and reactive VLA policies, listing action alignment, world-action factorisation, spatial and multi-view consistency, long-horizon memory, neural simulation for closed-loop policy learning and efficient inference as open challenges [33]. Scaling data is not a solution on its own: one 2026 study on 30,000 hours of ego-centric video reports that increasing training data 100x improves agent and object fidelity "unevenly" — the agent is modelled well while object fidelity "remains far lower and improves slowly", with folding paper rendered in the wrong configuration throughout even when hands are placed correctly [34].

## Theme 4: Autonomous driving
Driving world models are the most industrially developed application and the one with the clearest taxonomy: a 2025 survey categorises approaches by the modality of the predicted scene — semantic 2D images, 3D geometry/occupancy, or "scene-free" latent and multi-agent behaviour prediction — and reviews the datasets and metrics used for each [35]. Generation-oriented systems such as GAIA-1 and GAIA-2 operate as controllable synthetic-data and scenario generators conditioned on actions or text [7][27], and the new field of driving benchmarks has started to police them: DrivingGen defines driving world models as "generative simulators that imagine ego and agent futures" and benchmarks 14 state-of-the-art models with metrics for visual realism, trajectory plausibility, temporal coherence and controllability, finding a systematic trade-off in which "general models look better but break physics, while driving-specific ones capture motion realistically but lag in visual quality" [36]. An alternative is to reuse feature-space world models rather than pixels. AD-E2E-JEPA first evaluates existing action-conditioned JEPA world models (LeWM, DINO-WM, JEPA-WM) for end-to-end driving in a goal-conditioned zero-shot planning setting that uses ground-truth future observations as goals and trains no driving policy; it reports reaching goals 20 metres away within average displacements of 4.0 and 2.8 metres with trajectory vocabularies of 256 and 8,192 candidates, and 67.3/72.9 EPDMS on NAVSIMv2 with multiplicative safety metrics (84.1/86.5 EPDMS without them) [37]. Drive-JEPA adapts V-JEPA for driving, pre-training an encoder on large-scale driving video and adding a proposal-centric planner that distils simulator-generated and human trajectories with momentum-aware selection, reporting 93.3 PDMS on NAVSIM v1 and 87.8 EPDMS on v2 [38]. The caveat for cross-paper comparison is that these headline numbers use different metric variants (PDMS versus EPDMS, with and without multiplicative safety terms) even within the same benchmark family, so the reported improvements are not directly commensurable from the retrieved abstracts [37][38].

## Theme 5: Evaluation and benchmarks
Evaluation splits cleanly by tradition. In the control tradition the metric is downstream task return or success under a fixed budget: DreamerV3's journal protocol reports episode return across 8 domains and over 150 tasks under fixed hyperparameters, outperforming PPO in all domains while noting that baseline comparison budgets differ across methods [11], and embodied world models are evaluated on success rates in suites such as CALVIN [10] or by real-robot task completion [13][9]. In the generative tradition, the 2024-2026 answer has been purpose-built physics benchmarks. WorldModelBench crowd-sources 67K human labels to measure 14 frontier models, and adds instruction-following and physics-adherence dimensions so that subtle violations — the abstract's example is "irregular changes in object size that breach the mass conservation law" — are detected [39]. VideoPhy evaluates text-to-video models with separate Semantic Adherence and Physical Commonsense metrics and finds the best model, CogVideoX-5B, adheres to caption and physical laws in only 39.6% of instances, with the remaining models below 20% [14]. WorldScore unifies 3D/4D scene generation and video generation into next-scene generation tasks with explicit camera trajectories, scoring controllability, quality and dynamics, and reports that video models lack camera controllability — the best video model scored 40.22, below every 3D/4D generation model — with texture flicker as a recurrent consistency failure [40]. The underlying metrics have themselves been attacked: JEDi documents three limitations of FVD (non-Gaussian I3D feature space, insensitivity to temporal distortions, impractical sample sizes) and argues FVD is biased toward image quality and overlooks motion collapse [41], while WorldJen argues SSIM/PSNR "reward pixel fidelity over semantic correctness", that FVD "favors distributional textures over physical plausibility", and that binary VQA-based benchmarks are prone to yes-bias and low-resolution auditing [42]. Contact-level evaluation is still nascent: ChronoDreamer scores predicted rollouts with a vision-language model that reasons about collision likelihood, enabling rejection sampling of unsafe actions before execution [32]. Two 2026 meta-reviews generalise the diagnosis: a survey of 160 web-verified benchmarks from 2017 to 2026 sorts them into policy suites, embodied agents, world-model evaluation and prediction-to-action bridges, and finds that "world-model benchmarks score prediction without ever executing it", that only 11 (7%) build an explicit VLA-versus-world-model contrast, and that only four benchmarks turn prediction into executed action [15]; an evaluation-centric survey of 102 benchmarks from 2018 to 2026 characterises them along evaluation target, protocol, metrics and data, and concludes that targets are expanding toward functional utility while protocols, metrics and data lag behind — an evidence gap that "allows open-loop prediction scores to be overinterpreted as evidence of intervention response or closed-loop reliability beyond what a benchmark directly tests" [16]. Survey authors in both traditions converge on the same recommendation: measure physical consistency rather than pixel fidelity [26], and align prediction metrics with executed behaviour [15][16].

## Trends and open problems
Three shifts define the last two years. First, interaction has become the selling point: the frontier has moved from offline prediction to action-controllable, streaming and multi-agent generation systems (Genie, GameNGen, SPW-Nav, ME-World, Wonder) [4][5][29][30][31], and driving generation has matured into conditioned multi-camera simulation with its own benchmark [27][36]. Second, the prediction target has diversified away from pixels: latent states [2][3], task-relevant latents [24][12], pre-trained visual features [8][9], tokens [7] and diffusion latents [28][22] are now competing choices, and the 2025 embodied survey formalises this as an axis of spatial representation [26]. Third, evaluation and definition are being retrofitted: multiple surveys now exist solely to impose taxonomy [19][20][21] or to audit benchmarks [15][16], which is itself evidence that claims have outrun measurement.

The unsolved problems are consistent across sources. Compounding error in long rollouts remains the canonical failure — named as an exploitation of single-step generalisation as early as 2020 [43] and still named as "the core modeling difficulty" of long-horizon consistency [26]; a 2026 study finds that ensemble disagreement in latent dynamics models "does not reliably reflect global compounding model error accumulated over prolonged RSSM latent rollouts" and that imagined attractor regions can inflate returns and reinforce behaviour that does not correspond to real outcomes, i.e. learned-model exploitation [44]. Robustness to dynamics shift is related: even minor changes such as a body's mass or a floor's friction can degrade next-state prediction and, through compounding rollout errors, the policies built on top of them [25]. Physics fidelity is claimed more often than measured — Sora's report is explicit that it fails basic interactions [6], Cosmos' "accurate physics" is qualitative [22], and physics-adherence measurements put even the best systems below half of tested cases [14]. Object- and contact-level fidelity lags agent fidelity under data scaling [34], and contact mechanics is only beginning to be scored, through a vision-language collision judge rather than a contact-physics metric [32]. Finally, action coupling is an open engineering and scientific problem: World-Action models must decide how world prediction and action generation are factorised and aligned [33], and the benchmark literature shows that most evaluations still test prediction and control in isolation [15][16]. One disputed or at least unreconciled point deserves flagging: the sources retrieved for this survey disagree on how much physics-adherence measurement currently exists — video-generation physics benchmarks report low adherence [14][39], whereas the evaluation-audit surveys claim the evaluation targets have moved toward functional utility faster than the protocols and data to test it [16][15]; both can be true only if physics scores are being computed on benchmarks that were not designed to be intervention-faithful.
