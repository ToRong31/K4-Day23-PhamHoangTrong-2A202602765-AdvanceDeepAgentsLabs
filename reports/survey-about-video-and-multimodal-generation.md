# Video and Multimodal Generation: Architectures, Unified Models, Controllability, Evaluation and Data

## TL;DR
- Video generation has converged on latent diffusion with transformer (DiT) backbones operating over spacetime patches [1][2][3], while flow matching / rectified flow is the emerging training objective [4] and few-step distillation converts bidirectional teachers into causal streaming generators [5].
- Unified multimodal models divide into three design families - discrete-token early fusion [6], hybrid next-token-plus-diffusion objectives [7][8], and decoupled visual encoders [9] - and the 2026 frontier reframes the core open problem as misaligned representation spaces between understanding and generation [10].
- Evaluation is the weakest part of the stack: FVD has three documented structural limits [11] and CLIP-style alignment metrics misread temporal motion [12], so suites that decompose quality into human-annotated dimensions now dominate reporting [13][14][15].
- Controllability has widened from text conditioning [1] to semantic camera control [16], interactive navigable world models [17] and audio-video generation with streaming inference [18][19], but the only cross-system comparison retrieved is vendor-run [18].
- Data scale keeps growing - LVD-2M's 2M long-take clips with temporally dense captions [20] and 14M video-text pairs in 2025 [21] - yet no retrieved source gives a head-to-head quantitative comparison across all architecture families on shared benchmarks [11].

## Background
Video and multimodal generation covers generative models that synthesise temporally coherent video, and models that handle text, image, audio and video within a single architecture. Recent survey evidence describes the field's trajectory as a move "from adversarial models to diffusion-based models, yielding higher-fidelity, temporally consistent outputs", with alignment, long-range coherence and computational efficiency still listed as persistent challenges [22]. The foundational design choice is compression: latent diffusion avoids excessive compute by training the generative model in a compressed lower-dimensional latent space [3], and the same idea underlies contemporary video foundation models [1]. The second foundational choice is the denoising backbone: transformers over latent patches replaced the U-Net [2], and this recipe is now the default scaling path for video [1]. The topic matters now because the same architectural stack is being extended in two directions at once - toward unified any-to-any multimodal models [6][7][9] and toward interactive, real-time generation with audio [17][18][19] - while the measurement tools used to justify progress are themselves being challenged [11][12].

## Theme 1 - Backbone and objective families for video generation
The latent-video-diffusion family is anchored by Video LDM, which pre-trains an image latent diffusion model and then "turn[s] the image generator into a video generator by introducing a temporal dimension to the latent space diffusion model and fine-tuning on encoded image sequences", with diffusion upsamplers temporally aligned for high-resolution output [3]. Modern systems keep this decomposition but replace the backbone: Sora works "by first compressing videos into a lower-dimensional latent space, and subsequently decomposing the representation into spacetime patches", states that it "is a diffusion transformer", and claims that "diffusion transformers scale effectively as video models" [1]. The transformer-over-U-Net argument has a quantitative antecedent in the image domain: DiT replaces "the commonly-used U-Net backbone with a transformer that operates on latent patches", reports that higher-Gflops DiTs consistently have lower FID (DiT-XL/2 reaching a state-of-the-art FID of 2.27 on ImageNet 256x256), and presents DiT-XL/2 as compute-efficient and outperforming prior U-Net-based diffusion models such as ADM and LDM [2].

The training objective is where the families actually diverge. Rectified flow, which "connects data and noise in a straight line", is presented with "better theoretical properties and conceptual simplicity", although the authors of that scaling study concede it was "not yet decisively established as standard practice" alongside the incumbent diffusion formulation used for "images and videos" [4]. A 2025 text-to-video survey frames the same shift historically rather than as a clean replacement, again naming alignment, long-range coherence and efficiency as the unresolved gaps across families [22]. The autoregressive/token-based branch is best read as a composition with diffusion rather than a rival: CausVid adapts a pretrained bidirectional diffusion transformer into "an autoregressive transformer that generates frames on-the-fly" with KV caching, distils a bidirectional teacher into a causal student with distribution matching distillation, and reports 84.27 on VBench-Long at 9.4 FPS [5]. The evidence for "which family dominates" is programmatic rather than comparative - the most-cited video report explicitly omits implementation details and offers qualitative evaluation only [1], and no retrieved source benchmarks latent versus pixel space, U-Net versus DiT, and bidirectional versus causal autoregression on a shared protocol [11].

## Theme 2 - Unified and any-to-any multimodal generation
Three designs compete. The discrete-token, early-fusion route represents both modalities as tokens in one transformer, exemplified by Chameleon's "early-fusion token-based mixed-modal models capable of understanding and generating images and text in any arbitrary sequence", which required a stable-from-inception training approach, an alignment recipe and an architecture parameterisation tailored to that setting [6]. The hybrid-objective route keeps language modelling discrete and images continuous: Transfusion "combines the language modeling loss function (next token prediction) with diffusion to train a single transformer over mixed-modality sequences" and reports that it "scales significantly better than quantizing images and training a language model over discrete image tokens" [7]. Show-o occupies a middle position, unifying autoregressive text modelling with MaskGIT-style discrete diffusion over image tokens in one transformer, reporting roughly 20x fewer sampling steps than autoregressive image generation with understanding "comparable" to a LLaVA-v1.5-Phi-1.5 baseline [8]. The third route decouples the encoders: Janus uses "two independent visual encoding pathways: one for multimodal understanding and one for multimodal generation, unified by the same transformer architecture" (SigLIP for understanding, a VQ tokenizer for generation), and its ablation argues that a single shared encoder "made trade-offs between multimodal understanding and generation, ultimately sacrificing its multimodal understanding capability" [9]. The 2026 literature reframes these as symptoms of one problem: optimising understanding with sparse text signals while generating with dense pixel objectives "yields misaligned representation spaces, isolating visual understanding from generation and hindering their mutual reinforcement", with image segmentation proposed as a generative proxy to re-align both [10]. The comparison therefore rests on trade-off claims - quality versus sampling cost [8], continuous versus discrete tokenisation [7], capacity sharing versus understanding retention [9] - all reported qualitatively in the retrieved sources, and none of the retrieved unified models reports audio or video modalities with numbers [6][7][9].

## Theme 3 - Controllability, conditioning and interactive world models
Control surfaces have expanded from text to arbitrary conditioning. Sora is trained jointly on videos and images of variable duration, resolution and aspect ratio, and "can also be prompted with other inputs, such as pre-existing images or video", enabling editing tasks such as "creating perfectly looping video, animating static images, extending videos forwards or backwards in time" [1]. Semantic camera control is a newer, more relational task: SemCam defines "semantic camera motion control, a novel video-to-video task in which a reference video and a target motion label specify" the desired camera-subject relation, arguing that existing camera-controlled methods "typically rely on explicit trajectories or reference motions, which do not directly express these dynamic camera-subject relationships" [16]. Interactive generation is the most distinct branch: Genie 3 states that "given a text prompt," it "can generate dynamic worlds that you can navigate in real time at 24 frames per second, retaining consistency for a few minutes at a resolution of 720p", i.e. real-time navigable world simulation rather than clip-level synthesis [17]. Multimodal control also now includes audio: Veo 3 "is a video generation system capable of synthesizing high-quality, high-resolution video with audio from a text prompt or input image" and applies latent diffusion to "the temporal audio latents, and the spatio-temporal video latents" [18]. A 2026 survey of post-training and alignment supplies the unifying framing, observing that "pretrained video models often fail to reliably follow human intent, maintain temporal coherence, or satisfy physical and safety constraints", with alignment-specific difficulties in "error accumulation over time, motion-appearance coupling, multi-objective trade-offs, and limited supervision" [23]. The comparison problem is acute here: the retrieved cross-system evidence is a single vendor model card whose evaluation is run on Meta's MovieGenBench (1,003 prompts for video; 527 for video+audio) rather than on a shared open protocol, and the human-rating results it reports could not be retrieved for independent inspection [18], while the most-cited foundation report publishes no implementation details and only qualitative evaluation [1].

## Theme 4 - Efficiency, streaming and joint audio-video generation
Efficiency has moved from post-hoc acceleration to architectural design. CausVid adapts a pretrained bidirectional transformer into an autoregressive generator by "distilling 50-step diffusion model into a 4-step generator" with an asymmetric distillation strategy, enabling "streaming generation of high-quality videos at 9.4 FPS on a single GPU", and reports long-duration synthesis despite training on short clips [5]. Joint audio-video work pushes further: deploying a "joint audio-video diffusion transformer for real-time, interactive generation" is argued to require "block-autoregressive attention, so frames can be emitted before the whole clip is finished, and few-step sampling", and the authors contrast the conventional chained pipeline (distil a bidirectional teacher into a causal student, then a few-step one) with parallel adapter composition that avoids sequential fine-tuning [19]. Veo 3 extends the same latent formulation to both audio and video latents in a released system [18]. The pattern across these sources is that causal/block-autoregressive emission plus few-step sampling is now treated as a prerequisite for interactive use [5][19], and that a dedicated benchmark for joint audio-video generation has not been retrieved, leaving this frontier measured mostly by method-side claims [19].

## Theme 5 - Evaluation, metrics and benchmark suites
The two classic metric families both come with documented defects. FVD builds on FID's principles but introduces "a different feature representation that captures the temporal coherence of a video, in addition to the quality of each frame" using a Kinetics-trained I3D network, and reports a large-scale human study supporting its correlation with human judgement, together with the StarCraft 2 Videos benchmark [24]. CLIPScore is reference-free and "achieves the highest correlation with human judgements, outperforming existing reference-based metrics like CIDEr and SPICE", though it is "relatively weaker" for captions needing richer contextual knowledge such as news captions - and it was validated for image captioning, not video [25]. Later work attacks both. Beyond FVD identifies "three significant limitations": non-Gaussianity of the I3D feature space, insensitivity of I3D features to temporal distortions, and impractical sample sizes for reliable estimation, concluding FVD falls short as a standalone metric; its proposed JEDi replacement needs "only 16% of the samples to reach its steady value, while increasing alignment with human evaluation by 34%, on average" [11]. T2VScore argues that FVD and Video IS are "unsuitable for open-domain video generation due to their Full-Reference nature", while CLIP Score "computes an average of per-frame text-image similarities using image CLIP models, overlooking important temporal motion changes", producing "a mismatch between these objective metrics and human perception"; on its TVGE set of human judgements over 2,543 generated videos, objective metrics show low correlation with human text-video alignment [12].

The field's response is decomposition rather than a new scalar. VBench dissects "video generation quality" into 16 dimensions with tailored prompts and evaluation methods, argues that single-number metrics such as FVD "oversimplify" evaluation, and reports that its per-dimension results are highly correlated with collected human preference annotations [13]. VBench++ keeps the same 16 "specific, hierarchical, and disentangled dimensions" with fine-grained metrics and human preference annotations [14], while VBench-2.0 shifts the target to "intrinsic faithfulness" with five dimensions - "Human Fidelity, Controllability, Creativity, Physics, and Commonsense" - and a framework integrating VLMs/LLMs, anomaly-detection specialists and human preference annotations [15]. Two caveats follow from the retrieved text: the human-alignment claims are made by the benchmark authors about their own dimensions [13][15][11], and the same decomposed suites still borrow legacy assets such as UCF-101 class labels and MSR-VTT captions for prompt construction [13].

## Theme 6 - Data foundations
Scale growth is concentrated in caption quality and clip length rather than raw counts. LVD-2M curates "the first long-take video dataset ... comprising 2 million long-take videos, each covering more than 10 seconds and annotated with temporally dense captions", positioned against earlier corpora such as Panda-70M, HD-VG and WebVid in its dataset comparison [20]. A 2025 successor, ViMix-14M, provides "14 million video-text pairs" with improved captions and crawl-free access [21]. Meanwhile the evaluation side still leans on older assets - Kinetics-trained I3D features and StarCraft videos in FVD [24], UCF-101 and MSR-VTT prompts in VBench [13] - and no retrieved source quantifies dataset filtering or contamination effects on benchmark validity [20][21].

## Trends and open problems
Convergence on one scaling recipe is the clearest trend: latent compression [3], a transformer backbone over latent patches [2], and a diffusion or rectified-flow objective [1][4] now define the default path, with causal/autoregressive variants used to obtain streaming rather than to replace diffusion [5][19]. Flow matching is the objective most likely to displace plain diffusion, but the retrieved source that advocates it declines to call it standard practice [4], and no retrieved study compares objectives on a shared video protocol [11]. Unified multimodal generation is unresolved: discrete-token, hybrid and decoupled-encoder designs each trade image quality, sampling cost and understanding retention [6][7][8][9], and the 2026 diagnosis of misaligned representation spaces between understanding and generation [10] is the strongest statement of the underlying problem but is supported only qualitatively. Evaluation remains actively contested, with FVD and CLIP-based metrics shown to misread motion and distribution structure [11][12], and the remedy - decomposed benchmark suites with human preference annotations [13][14][15] - inherits a self-reporting bias because alignment is asserted by the benchmark authors. Controllability is increasingly framed as post-training and alignment, with error accumulation over time and motion-appearance coupling named as unsolved [23], while new control surfaces such as semantic camera relations [16] and interactive navigable worlds [17] lack shared evaluation. Independent comparison is the largest structural gap: the only cross-system comparison retrieved is a vendor-run evaluation on Meta's MovieGenBench [18], the most-cited foundation system publishes no details and no metrics [1], and this survey found no source that jointly evaluates architecture families, controllability and audio-video quality on one protocol [11][19]. Several claims survive only in weakened form: the Genie 3 blog's limitations section, the Veo 3 model card's human-rating table, and LVD-2M's corpus-composition figures sit beyond the retrieved text, so this survey asserts only their verifiable elements and marks the rest unverified here [17][18][20].
