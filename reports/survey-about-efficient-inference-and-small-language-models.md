# Efficient Inference and Small Language Models: Compression, Serving Systems, and Measured Limits

## TL;DR
- Inference efficiency is now pursued along three largely independent axes: bit-width reduction of weights/activations and of the KV cache [1][2][3][4][5], KV-memory and decoding-schedule engineering in serving systems [6][7][8][9], and IO-aware kernels for attention [10].
- The "small language model" line is defined by deployment economics rather than a fixed parameter cut-off — models built to run on-device, mobile and edge [11] — and its training recipes increasingly substitute data quality (diversity plus syntheticity) for raw parameter count [12][13].
- Tiny-model scaling evidence is non-monotonic: pass@1 improves from 0.224 at 135M to 0.707 at 7B, with a stability transition in the 1–1.5B range and diminishing returns beyond 3B [14], while a 3.8B model with heavily filtered and synthetic data reports 69% MMLU and phone deployment [15].
- Serving gains come mostly from memory, not math: PagedAttention targets a KV cache that "can be significantly wasted by fragmentation and redundant duplication" and raises throughput 2–4× at equal latency versus FasterTransformer and Orca [6], while draft-and-verify decoding accelerates output 2–3× losslessly [8] and trained drafters push this to up to 6.5× [9].
- Compression is not uniformly free: a 72-model SLM benchmark concludes that "quantization preserves reasoning capability, while pruning significantly disrupts it" [16], and small models' distinctive weakness is context reasoning rather than factual recall [17].

## Background
Efficient inference studies how to run a transformer more cheaply per token, and small language models (SLMs) study how to make the model itself smaller without losing usefulness; the two lines meet because most efficiency levers — quantization, pruning, distillation, batching, KV-cache management — are applied to the same serving stack. The foundational results are now several years old: 8-bit matrix multiplication halved inference memory while "retaining full precision performance" for models up to 175B [1], attention was reformulated as an IO-aware, tiled computation [10], output was accelerated by draft-and-verify decoding that "comput[es] several tokens in parallel" without changing the output distribution [8], and distillation showed that a smaller model can "retain most of the language understanding capabilities of larger models" [18]. What is new is the economics: SLMs "have become increasingly important due to their efficiency and performance to perform various language tasks with minimal computational resources, making them ideal for various settings including on-device, mobile, edge devices" [11], and compression is treated in that survey as a first-class component alongside architectures and training [11].

## Theme 1: Bit-width reduction — from 8-bit parity to sub-2-bit frontiers
The quantization literature splits along two design choices that the retrieved sources make explicit: what is quantized (weights only versus weights and activations) and how (post-training quantization, PTQ, versus quantization-aware training, QAT), with PTQ needing "no retraining of the original model" [19]. On the conservative end, INT8 works because the hard part is not weights but activations: SmoothQuant observes that "weights are easy to quantize while activations are not" and enables W8A8 by offline migration of quantization difficulty from activations to weights, making the scheme training-free [2]; independent evaluation of compression methods supports this direction, concluding that "quantization preserves reasoning capability" [16]. The weight-only PTQ lineage that followed is motivated by sheer storage pressure — GPT/OPT-class models have "extremely high computational and storage costs" and GPTQ is framed as compression for exactly that [4] — and AWQ refines it by exploiting non-uniform importance: "not all weights in an LLM are equally important" and "protecting only 1% salient weights can greatly reduce quantization error" [5].

Where these methods converge is that the reliable wins are at 8-bit and aggressive 4-bit weight-only settings; where they diverge is how far below 4 bits the evidence supports. BitNet b1.58 pushes to ternary weights, "in which every single parameter (or weight) of the LLM is ternary {-1, 0, 1}", and claims parity in "both perplexity and end-task performance" with an FP16 model of the same size and token budget while being "significantly more cost-effective in terms of latency, memory, throughput, and energy consumption" [20]. That parity claim is a training-time claim for a purpose-built architecture, not a drop-in compression result, and the retrieved evaluation evidence points the other way for naive low-bit compression: KV-cache vector quantization is attractive for aggressive long-context compression but "existing VQ methods degrade substantially in the 1-bit regime" [3]. The practical reading is that bit-width reduction is a spectrum with a well-verified 8-bit plateau, a contested 2–4-bit band, and a research frontier below 2 bits that currently requires either dedicated training [20] or method-specific fixes to the quantization space [3]. Notably, the captured evidence for the frontier (and for the classic PTQ methods) is abstract-level, so headline parity and degradation claims are largely self-reported rather than independently reproduced [20][3][4][5].

## Theme 2: How small models are trained — data curation instead of parameter count
The SLM literature shares a bet that data composition substitutes for scale. TinyStories is the canonical demonstration that synthetic, simplified data enables coherence at absurdly small scale: models "below 10 million total parameters, or ... with only one transformer block, yet still produce fluent and consistent stories", trained on text "generated by GPT-3.5 and GPT-4" and graded by GPT-4 [12]. The scaling-law follow-up turns this into a quantitative lever by adding an "effective training tokens" term defined as a "combination of ... (i) text diversity and (ii) syntheticity as measured by a teacher model", fitting constants over 200 models of 25M to 1.5B parameters and reporting a "+0.83 Pearson correlation with true accuracies" across eight reasoning tasks [13]. Production-grade recipes instantiate the same idea at larger budgets: phi-3-mini is a "3.8 billion parameter language model trained on 3.3 trillion tokens" whose data mixes "heavily filtered publicly available web data and synthetic data", and it "rivals that of models such as Mixtral 8x7B and GPT-3.5" while being "small enough to be deployed on a phone", with 69% MMLU and 8.38 MT-bench [15]. A decade-earlier comparison point shows how much of this is a data rather than architecture story: DistilBERT already showed a distilled model could "retain most of the language understanding capabilities of larger models" [18], but the modern SLM claim is about matching models 10–50× larger on broad benchmarks [15], which the older distillation result did not attempt.

The second cluster of SLM work is post-hoc: take a small dense model and post-train it hard. VibeThinker-3B is a 3B dense model improved by a "pipeline that includes curriculum-based supervised fine-tuning, multi-domain reinforcement learning, and offline self-distillation", reporting 94.3 on AIME26 and 80.2 Pass@1 on LiveCodeBench v6 and "matching or exceeding flagship models that are orders of magnitude larger" [21]. Crucially, that paper bounds its own claim: it proposes a "Parametric Compression-Coverage Hypothesis" under which "verifiable reasoning [is] compressible into compact reasoning cores, while open-domain knowledge ... require[s] broad parameter coverage" [21] — a hypothesis about where small models can win, exactly matching the benchmark split between verifiable-reasoning tasks and context-heavy evaluation found elsewhere [21][16][17]. Application-specific SLMs occupy a third niche: SlimLM targets on-device document assistance and offers itself as "a benchmark for on-device language models" [22], while the SLM survey frames the whole space as architectures plus training plus compression with an accompanying taxonomy and benchmark discussion [11].

## Theme 3: Serving systems — KV memory dominates, decoding schedule second
Measurements across the serving literature agree that KV-cache memory, not FLOPs, is the binding constraint at inference time. PagedAttention states the problem as KV memory that "can be significantly wasted by fragmentation and redundant duplication" and addresses it with "an attention algorithm inspired by the classical virtual memory and paging techniques in operating systems", which lets vLLM "improve the throughput of popular LLMs by 2-4× with the same level of latency compared to the state-of-the-art systems, such as FasterTransformer and Orca" [6]. The project's own write-up repeats the diagnosis more aggressively — existing systems "waste 60% - 80% of memory due to fragmentation and over-reservation" — and quotes "up to 24x higher throughput compared to HF and up to 3.5x higher throughput than TGI" [7]; that source is a vendor blog benchmark on specific GPUs, so it should be read as an upper bound rather than an independent result [7].

Decoding-schedule work attacks the sequential dependency instead. Speculative decoding established the template: run the target "in parallel on the outputs of the approximation models ... without changing the distribution", demonstrated on T5-XXL with a "2X-3X acceleration ... with identical outputs" and no retraining [8]. The recent evolution moves the drafter from a generic small model to a trained, feature-fusing component: EAGLE-3 "abandons feature prediction in favor of direct token prediction" and applies multi-layer feature fusion via "training-time test", reporting "a speedup ratio up to 6.5x, with about 1.4x improvement over EAGLE-2", and 1.38× throughput improvement at batch size 64 inside SGLang [9]. Kernel-level work is complementary rather than competing: FlashAttention reduces "the number of memory reads/writes between GPU high bandwidth memory (HBM) and GPU on-chip SRAM" via tiling, giving a "15% end-to-end wall-clock speedup on BERT-large (seq. length 512)", "3× speedup on GPT-2 (seq. length 1K)" and 2.4× on Long-Range Arena, and enabling 16K–64K contexts [10].

The most recent captured framing argues these strands are one problem: KV eviction and quantization, prompt pruning/distillation, bounding architectural state, and agent-memory consolidation are all "a rate–distortion decision about what context-derived information to retain versus discard, at what fidelity, under a resource budget" [23]. It also supplies the sharpest critique available in the retrieved set: the retention signal is typically "attention magnitude or recency, and it fails in the same way everywhere, by discarding ... information the query later needs", and repeated agent compaction is "almost never measured" [23]. That tension — paging and speculative decoding optimize throughput against a fixed retention policy that may itself be wrong — is the most interesting unresolved comparison in this literature [6][8][23][9].

## Theme 4: Deployment — runtime formats, edge backends, and where the evidence stops
On-device deployment is where quantization meets hardware. llama.cpp is the reference implementation in the retrieved evidence: it supports "1.5-bit, 2-bit, 3-bit, 4-bit, 5-bit, 6-bit, and 8-bit integer quantization" and targets edge backends including "Hexagon | Snapdragon", "OpenCL | Adreno GPU", "Metal | Apple Silicon" and "CANN | Ascend NPU", plus "CPU+GPU hybrid inference to partially accelerate models larger than the total VRAM capacity" [24]. Model-side evidence for phone-class deployment comes from phi-3-mini's 3.8B/3.3T-token recipe explicitly positioned as "locally on your phone" [15] and from SlimLM's application-specific on-device document assistant [22]. The trade-offs are asymmetric across scales: for edge reasoning workloads, "semantic reliability per unit edge resource does not scale monotonically with parameter count", with mid-scale "approximately 1.5 to 3B" models identified as most favourable [14]. What is missing is measurement rather than capability: the runtime sources document supported formats and backends but provide documentation-level size tables rather than independent latency or energy figures on a named phone SoC [24], and the SLM evaluation literature concentrates on accuracy rather than joules per token [16][17].

## Theme 5: Evaluation evidence — quantization is safe, pruning is not, distillation helps little
Independent, large-scale evaluation is the scarcest resource in this area, and the one captured benchmark that covers the production compression methods jointly is unusually informative: it evaluates "72 diverse SLMs from six major model families across 17 reasoning benchmarks" [16]. Its headline conclusion cuts across the compression taxonomy of Theme 1 and the deployment formats of Theme 4: "quantization preserves reasoning capability, while pruning significantly disrupts it" [16]. That is a direct counterweight to method papers reporting near-lossless compression [2][5] and to training-from-scratch pruning alternatives designed for inference efficiency and task transferability, such as MaskLLM's learnable "N:M sparsity" via Gumbel-Softmax sampling [25], whose accuracy and speedup deltas were not captured in retrieved text [25].

Where small models fail is a separate question from how well they compress, and the captured evidence points at context handling rather than at factual recall. OnionEval restricts its scope to models "with fewer than 10 billion parameters" and introduces a "multi-layer structured framework with a specific metric called the context-influence score (CI)" that probes hallucination as context is wrapped in progressively deeper layers [17]; the paper's reported pattern is that small models are more dependable on local factual questions than on context-dependent reasoning, with Chain-of-Thought prompting mitigating the gap, but the per-model accuracy and CI values recorded while drafting this survey could not be re-verified against the fetched source page and are therefore not reproduced here [17]. This pattern lines up with the scale-dependent picture from edge reasoning benchmarks, where gains are non-uniform and mid-scale models are preferred per unit resource [14], and with the narrower claim that compact models can match frontier performance specifically on verifiable reasoning [21]. Taken together, the evidence supports a division of labour: 8-bit compression is supported by the foundational results [1][2], pruning needs per-task validation before deployment [16][25], and small models should be trusted for what is verifiable or factually local rather than for context-heavy reasoning [21][16][17].

## Trends and open problems
Three shifts are visible across the last two years of retrieved work. First, quantization has moved from making 8-bit lossless [1][2][4][5] toward inventing formats that are aggressive by construction — ternary weights [20] and 1-bit KV caches [3] — while evaluation evidence continues to price the risk at each step [3][16]. Second, serving optimization has moved from lossless verification of untrained drafters [8] toward trained drafters that fuse multi-layer features [9], and toward systems that treat all retained state as a single budgeted allocation problem [23]; the throughput claims are strong but are reported against specific baselines, hardware and batch sizes [6][7][9]. Third, SLM capability arguments have shifted from parameter count to data curation and post-training [12][13][21][15], with a growing consensus that the ceiling depends on the task family — verifiable reasoning compresses well, open-domain breadth does not [21][17].

What is unsolved and disputed: (i) the parity claims for sub-2-bit weights [20] are contested by evidence that vector quantization degrades "substantially in the 1-bit regime" [3] and by a 72-model benchmark finding that pruning significantly disrupts reasoning ability [16]; (ii) what distillation buys for reasoning remains unclear — the captured benchmark separates quantization from pruning but does not settle distillation [16], while a self-distilled 3B model claims frontier-level verifiable-reasoning scores [21]; (iii) retention policies inside KV/prompt compaction are known to fail on future-dependent information and are "almost never measured" over repeated compaction [23]; and (iv) the retrieved evidence base is thin in specific places — most method claims are abstract-level and self-reported [20][3][21][4][5], the headline throughput numbers come from paper-internal or vendor benchmarks [7][9], no controlled head-to-head bit-width-versus-accuracy table was captured [16][24], and the per-model numeric deltas of the 72-model SLM benchmark (exact GSM8K loss under 4-bit post-training quantization and under 50% weight removal) could not be re-verified from the fetched source text, only its qualitative conclusion [16]; two further numeric details recorded during drafting — the runtime's Q4_K_M size table for an 8B model and the serving blog's residual-waste percentage — were likewise unverifiable against the fetched pages and were therefore dropped from this report [24][7]. Citable gaps that remain open for follow-up work: the original scaling-law papers and the EAGLE-1 drafter were not retrievable in this pass, so the lineage above is anchored on the sources actually retrieved.
